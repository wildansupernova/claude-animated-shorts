# "The Snot Highway": script v1 (2026-11-15)
Format: ~65 s vertical short. Hook (paradox) → setting (truck, 7,500 lb of hagfish) → escalation (brakes, container flies, spill, panic) → snap (cars coated) → "Wait...?" → "You see," (slime glands, thread skeins, 10,000x in <0.5 s) + "snot airbag" analogy → sharks gag → bulldozer cleanup → payoff (300 million years of being disgusting).

## Facts (sources)
- 13 July 2017, U.S. 101 near Depoe Bay, Oregon: a truck with 13 containers, net 7,500 lb of hagfish ("slime eels"); the driver approached road construction and tried to stop; one container flew into the southbound lane and hit a car, setting off a five-vehicle crash with no serious injuries; the other containers spilled onto the highway. (Oregon State Police release via KLCC; OPB; CBS)
- The hagfish were shipped to Korea for food. (OSP; Atlas Obscura)
- Stressed hagfish release slime; photos show cars and the road coated in it. Depoe Bay Fire used a small bulldozer and firehoses; the highway reopened in the afternoon. (KLCC; OPB)
- Slime: protein threads tightly wound in tiny skeins unravel in seawater; the slime expands ~10,000x its volume in under half a second; it is ~99.996 % water. (KQED; Wikipedia "Hagfish")
- 2011 footage (Zintzen et al.) shows sharks and other predators biting hagfish and retreating, gagging, slime hanging from their gills. (National Geographic; Discover)
- Hagfish lineage ~300 million years old (older than dinosaurs, ~230 Myr). (Yahoo/Popular science; Wikipedia)
