# "The Horses and the Electric Eels": script v1 (2026-11-15)
Format: ~65 s vertical short. Hook (paradox: fish attacked horses, nobody believed it for 200 years) → setting (Humboldt, Venezuela, 1800) → escalation (horses driven into the eel pond, eels rise up and zap) → snap (two horses drown) → doubters → "Wait...?" → 2016 Catania, eels leap at his net → "You see," (current spreads in water vs. through you) + stadium analogy → leap height = shock strength → payoff (Catania lets an eel zap his own arm on purpose).

## Facts (sources)
- March 1800, near Calabozo, Venezuela: Alexander von Humboldt wanted to catch electric eels; local fishermen drove about 30 horses and mules into an eel pool; the eels pressed against the horses and discharged; two horses drowned within minutes, others collapsed; Humboldt published the account (1807). (Live Science 2016; CSMonitor; Science Friday; Mongabay)
- Colleagues dismissed the story as "poetically transfigured" / exaggerated for ~200 years. (Live Science; CSMonitor)
- 2016, PNAS: Kenneth Catania (Vanderbilt) noticed eels leaping out of the water at a metal-rimmed net; tests with a fake crocodile head showed the eel presses its chin on the target; the higher it leaps, the stronger the shock, because more current passes through the target instead of the water. (Live Science; Inside Science; CBC)
- 2017, Current Biology: Catania measured the current through his own arm from a small eel's shocking leap (40-50 mA peak); described it as like an electric fence. (ScienceDaily; NPR; National Geographic)
