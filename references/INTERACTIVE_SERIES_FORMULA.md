# Interactive "What would you do?" horror series: the formula

Breakdown of a viral two-part reel series (an animated "rules horror" story told in the second person, where every
episode ends on a numbered choice the audience votes on in the comments), turned into a repeatable formula for
our own series. The reference is analysed here only for structure. Never copy its names, characters, setting,
rules or lines (see "Make it ours" below).

## 1. The reference in numbers

| | Part 1 | Part 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 98.7 s | 113.1 s |
| Likes / comments | ~780 K / 8.4 K (1.08 % comment rate) | ~441 K / 3.8 K (0.85 %) |
| Posted | day 1 | +11 hours |
| Caption | "<Place> Part 1 #series #story #decide" | "<Place> Part 2 #story #decide #series" |
| Narration | 265 words, 161 wpm, 8.5 words per sentence | 323 words, 171 wpm, 7.3 words per sentence |
| "you/your" | 9 % of all words | 11 % of all words |
| Shot length | ~3.5 s (28 hard cuts) | ~2.4 s (47 hard cuts) |
| Mix | -14 LUFS, LRA 2.7 LU, music bed ~3 dB under voice | -14 LUFS, LRA 3.0 LU, quieter bed + hits |
| Ending | last word, then 1.3 s of picture. No outro, no "follow", no end card | same |

Part 2 kept 57 % of Part 1's likes, which is normal decay for a series. Part 1 is the funnel, so it gets the most
care.

Measured from comments: people answer with just the number ("2", "Stay"), argue loopholes ("board, because the
rule was only for staff") and joke ("I'd just fall asleep"). All three kinds count as comments, and comments
drive reach.

## 2. Beat map: Part 1 (the pilot)

| Time | % | Beat | What happens (paraphrased) | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-6 s | 6 % | **Hook = the trap** | "Imagine you..." + an everyday mishap that locks you in, in ONE sentence | Second person, relatable, stakes in 6 s |
| 6-23 s | 17 % | **Isolation stack** | Place name → alone → phone dead/no signal → next escape is hours away → "no choice but to stay" | Removes every way out: people, phone, time, exit |
| 24-40 s | 16 % | **The document** | You find a staff-only note: "Rule No. 1: <never do X>". The rest is torn off | A rule turns the story into a game the viewer can reason about. Missing rules = future episodes |
| 40-44 s | 4 % | **False relief** | "Probably a joke between coworkers" (the character smiles) | Drops tension so the next hit lands |
| 44-57 s | 13 % | **The event** | Clock hits midnight, every light goes out, something arrives | Breaks the normal world on a precise time |
| 58-68 s | 11 % | **Wrongness** | The arrival is wrong in 3 sensory details (silent, stops right at you, empty) and it is exactly what Rule 1 warned about | Makes obeying the rule feel urgent |
| 69-86 s | 17 % | **The presence** | Footsteps behind you, slow, then stop; breath on your neck | A second threat that makes obeying the rule dangerous |
| 86-88 s | 2 % | **Clock** | The doors start closing | Forces the decision NOW |
| 88-95 s | 7 % | **The choice** | "You have two choices: <break the rule> or <stay with the threat>" + numbered on-screen tags | Both options are bad = real debate |
| 95-99 s | 2 % | **Question** | "So, what would you do?" then a hard stop | Comment prompt; the abrupt end invites a rewatch |

Hidden extra: the note shows Rule No. 2 half-torn on screen ("If you hear footsteps behind you, do not...") while
the voice says the rest is missing. The footsteps then happen. People who pause and read get rewarded, and it
foreshadows the presence. It costs one prop texture and pays off in rewatches and comments.

## 3. Beat map: Part 2 (every sequel)

| Time | Beat | What happens | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-4 s | **Vote recap** | "So, did you <1> or <2>?" over Part 1's last frame with the tags | Re-hooks Part 1 viewers, onboards new ones |
| 4-38 s | **Losing branch** (~30 %) | "If you <2>..." The voice first validates the choice ("the note was a warning, and you followed it"), then reveals the threat fully (a face, a weapon), a short chase, help fails, "This is the end." | Everyone who picked it gets a vivid payoff. A death scene is the most rewatched part |
| 40-41 s | **"Let's rewind."** | Rewind glitch, VHS-style | Lets you show BOTH branches, so nobody feels ignored |
| 42-53 s | **Winning branch: relief** | "If you <1>..." You see the threat you escaped through the glass and finally breathe | Validates the other half of the voters |
| 53-68 s | **Twist** | The safe place goes wrong: speeds up, wrong tracks, unknown place, lights out | Escalates |
| 68-84 s | **Game reveal** | A screen: "Welcome to the <Game Name>". One minute to decide, two buttons | Reframes the series from survival to a game, which is what keeps it going |
| 84-97 s | **Stakes of both options** | "If you go, you don't know what's waiting. If you stay, this <thing> chose you for a reason." | Both bad again |
| 97-105 s | **Countdown** | The doors are open onto darkness, timer at 00:05 | Ticking-clock tension |
| 106-112 s | **New choice + question** | "Do you <1>... or do you <2>?" + new numbered tags. Hard stop | Next vote |

**Key sequel rules:**
- **The vote never blocks the writer.** Both branches play, the losing one dies, and canon goes on in the other.
  In the reference, most commenters picked the branch that died. So you can **write and render a whole season
  before posting Part 1**.
- **Canon = the branch that goes deeper into the unknown** (usually the one that breaks the rule). Obeying the rule
  leads to a quick, grounded, final ending. Breaking it opens the bigger world.
- **A branch badge** (pill at the top centre, e.g. "② STAY") stays on screen for the whole branch so viewers always
  know which timeline they're watching.
- **Each new dilemma escalates by one notch:** a timer appears, a voice speaks, the place is unknown, the stakes are
  personal ("chose you for a reason").

## 4. Why it works (the mechanisms)

1. **Second person + present tense.** "Imagine you..." makes the viewer the main character. The main character is
   a blank figure with no name, no voice and no backstory, so anyone can project onto it.
2. **A mishap everyone has had** (missed the last train) slides into the impossible. The first 6 s feel real.
3. **The isolation stack** answers every "why don't you just..." comment before anyone writes it: no people, no phone,
   no exit, no time.
4. **Rules give the audience something to reason about.** A numbered rule turns a scary story into a puzzle with
   right and wrong answers. A torn page gives the series more rules to reveal.
5. **Two threats that cancel each other out.** The rule says don't do X, and the presence makes staying deadly.
   Each option obeys one danger and walks into the other, so the comment section splits about 50/50 and people
   argue.
6. **Commenting takes no effort.** Options are numbered (1 blue, 2 red), so the whole comment can be one keystroke.
   Short labels (1-2 words) are pinned to the objects in the scene with leader lines.
7. **Precise, concrete details** (3 % battery, 23:42, next train 05:30, 23:59 on the clock) make it believable and
   give the cutter instant insert shots.
8. **A twist every 10-15 s.** "But then...", "And at that exact moment...", "But something is wrong." Never more
   than ~15 s without a new turn.
9. **The face does the acting.** The character is a simple 3D figure with a drawn-on cartoon face (eyes, brows,
   mouth). Every ~10 s there is a close-up with a new expression (annoyed, smug, worried, shocked). It reads
   instantly at phone size and is cheap to animate.
10. **The villain is the hero inverted.** It has the same body in black with glowing white eyes and an oversized
    grin. It can be read in silhouette and is iconic enough to be a thumbnail.
11. **Light and colour mark the act.** Grey/teal realism, then every light goes out (blue-black), then the game
    world (all orange-red). Every light change is a plot beat.
12. **Loud, flat mix** (-14 LUFS, ~3 LU range) and a music bed that never drops out, so there's no dead air.
13. **No outro.** It ends on the question with 1.3 s of picture. The loop restarts before the viewer decides to
    scroll.
14. **The sequel comes fast** (11 h later) while the vote is still hot in the comments.

## 5. The formula

> **Ordinary place + you get trapped + every escape removed + a rule you didn't write + a threat that makes the
> rule dangerous + two bad options, numbered = Part 1.**
> **Every later part = vote recap → losing branch dies → "Let's rewind" → winning branch survives into a bigger game
> → new timed choice.**

### 5.1 Series bible (fill this in once per series)

```
SERIES NAME:      <Place>                         (the post title is "<Place> Part N")
PLACE:            liminal + everyday: somewhere everyone has been alone at night
TRAP:             one-sentence mishap that locks you in ("you miss the last ___ and ___ slams shut")
ISOLATION STACK:  people ✗ | phone ✗ (battery %, no signal) | exit ✗ | time ✗ (next escape at HH:MM)
THE DOCUMENT:     who wrote it (staff / previous victim / the place itself), title, Rule 1, Rule 2 half-torn on screen
THE EVENT:        exact time + sensory break (lights out, sound stops, something arrives)
THE PRESENCE:     a threat revealed one sense per episode: sound → touch → face → weapon/intent
THE GAME:         the bigger system revealed in Part 2 ("Welcome to the ___"), with its own voice and screen
THE WHY:          the season mystery ("it chose you for a reason"), answered in the finale
SEASON:           5-8 parts; finale twist answers THE WHY (loop endings work: you become staff / you write the note)
```

### 5.4 Dilemma checklist (every choice must pass all of these)

- [ ] Exactly two options, 1-2 words each, numbered ①/②.
- [ ] Both are bad: one breaks a known rule, the other faces a known threat.
- [ ] Neither one is clearly correct. If you can predict an 80/20 split, rewrite it.
- [ ] Each option is a physical place or object the camera can point a tag at (a door, a button, the dark).
- [ ] A clock forces the choice now (doors closing, a timer, footsteps getting closer).
- [ ] There's a loophole to argue about (who the rule was for, what "empty" means, what the torn part said).

### 5.5 Script style rules

- Second person, present tense, from the first word. Never name the main character.
- Sentences of 6-10 words, ~165 wpm, 0.4-1.2 s pause after each sentence (room for a sound hit and a cut).
- One concrete number or time every ~15 s, and show it on a prop (phone, board, clock, timer).
- A turn word every 10-15 s: "But then", "And at that exact moment", "But something is wrong", "Then".
- Reveal the presence one sense per episode (sound → breath → face → intent). Never show it all at once.
- Last line is always a question that repeats both options. Nothing comes after it.

## 6. Picture, captions and sound specs

**Characters.**
- **"You":** one blank, neutral-coloured figure with a swappable drawn face (eyes, brows, mouth).
- **Expressions:** keep a fixed set of 6: neutral, annoyed, smug-smile, worried, shocked "O", terrified. Cut to a
  face close-up roughly every 10 s.
- **Threat:** the hero's silhouette inverted (dark body, glowing eyes, oversized grin). Hide it in darkness until
  the reveal episode.

**Shots.**
- Shot length ~3.5 s in Part 1, ~2.5 s in sequels.
- Mix of wides (the empty place), inserts (phone, board, note, clock, timer) and face close-ups.
- Use slow push-ins for dread and a fast snap to the face on a reveal. Pick moves with the `camera-moves` skill.

**Light and colour.**
- Act 1: desaturated realism with one warm accent (LED board).
- "Lights out": blue-black with practical sources only.
- The game: one saturated colour bath (orange-red). Change the world colour when the rules of the world change.

**Captions.**
- 1-3 word chunks, bold uppercase sans, white with a soft dark shadow, centred.
- Position: lower middle (~69 % of frame height). Drop to ~84 % when a face or prop needs the centre.
- Adapt `videos-done/birthday-bet/src/videos/birthday/Captions.tsx`. This format needs captions, so it is an
  exception to the house "no captions" default.

**Choice tags.**
- Pill shape, dark fill, 2 px white outline, a numbered circle (① blue, ② red), and a thin leader line to a dot
  on the object in the scene.
- Tags pop in on the word "two choices" and stay to the end.
- In sequels, a white pill at the top centre shows the current branch.

**Recurring props** (become the series' brand): the rules note with Rule 2 half-legible, a dot-matrix time board,
the phone lock screen with battery % and "No Service", and the game screen with a countdown.

**Sound.**
- Continuous dark drone or pulse under everything.
- Hits on every reveal, footsteps and breathing as foley, a rewind whoosh for "Let's rewind".
- Mix to -14 LUFS with a loudness range of ~3 LU, voice on top.
- End on the last word plus ~1.3 s.

**Voice.** One calm, low narrator, slightly confidential, like telling a story at night. With Kokoro, pick one
male voice and keep it for the whole series.

### 6.1 Retention and comment rules (apply to every episode)

`videos-done/floor-zero-part-2/` has the code for the HUD, the cold-open teaser, the COMMENT pill and the vote banner
(`story.ts`, `Overlay.tsx`, `FloorZero2.tsx`). `videos-done/floor-zero-series/SEASON.md` is the worked season plan,
audited for both virality and story logic.

**Keep them watching.**
1. **Cold open (first ~1 s):** flash forward to the episode's strongest image, glitched, with a sting and a white
   flash into the recap (`TEASER`). No logo and no fade-in. Start the voice after it (`LEAD`).
2. **Open loop by 3 s:** the recap question is on screen with both numbered tags, so the viewer stays to see their
   branch.
3. **Something changes every 2-3 s:** a cut, an insert, a new camera move or a highlighted caption word. A held
   shot must still creep (a slow push), never sit dead.
4. **Plant, then pay off, three times:** show the clue early and in passing, and pay it off later in a way that
   changes its meaning. Floor Zero: the mirrored "UOY" badge in the steel (Part 3) and on CCTV (Part 4), a glimpse
   (Part 5), then the finale's "it learned you backwards". Never mark a clue by hand in a way the story can't explain.
5. **The losing branch escalates, but never answers the season's question.** Give each death one new sense of the
   threat (its voice, a hand on your shoulder, its breath). Keep the reveals that matter in the canon branch, because
   viewers learn that losing branches are dead ends.
6. **Every rule is true; the argument is the wording.** Don't make the rules lies: then every vote becomes "break it"
   and splits 80/20. Make the rules precise, and let viewers win by reading them (Floor Zero Part 1: zero lit up on the
   panel before the doors opened).
7. **A visible clock from the game reveal to the end:** a small countdown HUD below the top ~20 % of the frame. If
   story time jumps, make it a rule the viewer sees (Floor Zero: "the dark eats time", so the HUD jumps after each
   blackout).
8. **No dead air at the end:** the last line, then ~1.3 s. The last frame becomes the next part's recap frame.

**Get them commenting.**
1. **Ask for the number, at the end only:** a "COMMENT 1 OR 2 ↓" pill under the pinned tags during the final
   question. It must not appear earlier, because it breaks the spell.
2. **Leave one loophole per episode** that the episode doesn't answer ("NEVER LO…": look at it, or look away?).
3. **Show the vote back:** the next part's recap can show Part N-1's real split from the comments (`VOTES`). It stays
   `null` until real numbers exist. Never invent them.
4. **Canon goes to the branch that goes deeper**, not the majority, and the losing branch still gets its ending, so
   nobody's vote is wasted.
5. **Pin and reply:** pin the question as a comment, and reply to the first ~50 comments in-world (see §7).

## 7. Posting playbook

- Title: `<Series> Part N`. Hashtags: `#series #story #decide` plus 1-2 niche tags.
- Pin your own comment on Part 1: "① <option 1> or ② <option 2>? Part 2 tomorrow." (This is safe because the sequel
  shows both branches.)
- Post Part 2 within 12-24 h. Batch-render the whole season first, then queue it with the `video-workflow` skill
  (Buffer).
- Reply to the first ~50 comments with in-world replies ("the note said EMPTY train...") to keep the argument
  going.
- Watch: comment rate (aim for ≥1 % of likes), the 1/2 split (aim for 40-60 %), and Part 2 likes as a share of
  Part 1 (≥50 % means the series is holding).

## 8. Make it ours (originality rules)

- Use our own place, trap, rules, game name and lines. Don't reuse the reference's station, its rules, its game
  name, its mannequin hero or its grinning villain.
- Our hero can be the house bean-head card character (root `SKILL.md`) relit in a dark liminal palette. That's
  distinct, and it's already our brand.
- The genre is fair game: second-person rules horror, numbered choices, rewind sequels. The specific work is not.

## 10. Worked example: "Floor Zero"

**Part 1 voice-over** (≈255 words, ≈95 s at 165 wpm; tags ① STEP OUT / ② STAY IN):

> Imagine you work late, and you're the last person left in the entire building. You step into the elevator and
> press the ground floor. The doors close. Then the lights flicker, and the elevator starts going... up.
> You press the button again. Nothing. You check your phone. Two percent battery. No signal. You hit the emergency
> button. It just rings, and rings, and nobody answers.
> Then the elevator stops. The screen says a floor that isn't on the panel. Floor zero.
> But then you notice a card taped to the mirror. Building maintenance. After hours only. Rule number one. If the
> elevator stops at floor zero, never step out. The rest has been scratched off.
> You laugh, and think it's probably the night cleaners messing with people.
> Then, with a soft ding, the doors open. A dark hallway. Green carpet. One light buzzing at the very end.
> But something is wrong. The hallway is silent. No air conditioning. No traffic. Not even your own echo.
> At the end of the hall, a door opens, and a woman in a cleaner's uniform waves at you. She calls your name. You
> never told anyone here your name.
> And at that exact moment, above you, on the roof of the elevator, you hear knocking. Three slow knocks. Then
> silence. Then the ceiling panel creaks.
> The doors start to close.
> You have two choices. You step out to the woman who knows your name, or you stay in the elevator, with whatever
> is on the roof.
> So, what would you do?

On-screen Easter egg: the card's Rule 2 is half visible: "IF SOMETHING KNOCKS FROM ABOVE, DO NOT LO...".

**Part 2 outline.**
1. **Recap:** "So, did you step out, or did you stay in?"
2. **② Stay in:** The doors close, and the voice validates ("the card was a warning, and you followed it"). The
   knocking stops and the ceiling panel slides open. Rule 2 was "...do not look up", and you look up. "This is
   the end."
3. **"Let's rewind."**
4. **① Step out:** Behind you, the elevator plunges down the shaft with a scream of metal. You were right to leave.
   The woman smiles: "Finally. Your shift starts now."
5. **Twist:** Every office door in the hallway has a nameplate, and they're all people who "worked late". The last
   one is blank.
6. **Game reveal:** A PA voice: "Welcome to the Night Shift. Clean floor zero before 6 AM, or your name goes on
   the door." A timer reads 05:59:00.
7. **New choice:** Two doors are open. One has your boss's name; one has your best friend's. You hear someone
   crying behind both. Thirty seconds. ① Boss ② Friend. "So, which door do you open?"

## 11. Production notes for this repo

- Build it like the other 3D shorts (`templates/bridge/`, three.js + Remotion). One small set per series (a
  station, an elevator, a gas station) carries the whole season, so later episodes cost far less than Part 1.
- **Length** is 85-115 s, longer than the house 55-70 s. The format needs the isolation stack and the presence
  build. For a 60 s cut, trim the isolation stack to 2 items and the presence to 2 escalations. Keep every other
  beat.
- **Voice:** one Kokoro narrator for the whole series (`scripts/tts.py <voice> <speed>`) at ~165 wpm, with
  `words.json` driving the caption chunks and the tag pop on "two choices".
- **Reusable per series:** the rules-note texture, the choice-tag component, the branch badge, the rewind
  transition and the countdown screen. Build them once.
- **Record** each finished part in `videos-done/<series>-part-N/` and the index as usual.
