# Floor Zero, Season 1: series bible (v1, for audit)

Seven parts. Part 1 is posted and is fixed canon. Part 2 is rendered but not posted, so it can still change. Parts 3-7
are new. Everything below follows `references/INTERACTIVE_SERIES_FORMULA.md` plus the viral skills listed in §9.

## 0. Why the Part 2 "they were there to keep you" line goes
The line answered the season's central question in episode 2, and it told the viewer instead of showing them. Worse, it
made every later choice predictable: if the rules are a trap, you always break them, so the vote splits 80/20 and
nobody argues in the comments. That breaks our own dilemma checklist. It is cut (§8 lists the Part 2 edits).

The replacement engine: **every rule is true, and it means exactly what it says.** The rules aren't lies. They're
precise, and someone has scratched parts of them out. Viewers win by reading the wording. On the surface, sometimes
obeying wins and sometimes breaking wins, so no one can game it.

## 1. Logline and promise
You're the last one leaving the office. The elevator goes past the ground floor to floor zero, an exact copy of your
office, with someone in the corner facing the wall. Seven floors down, one rule on each, and you have to clock out before
06:00. Every floor is your office again, a little more wrong. And the one facing the wall keeps getting closer, and
keeps looking more like you.

## 2. The truth (hidden; revealed in parts 5 and 7)
1. **The Night Shift keeps one.** Every night the building takes the last one leaving. If they haven't clocked out on -7
   by 06:00, they are kept: drained grey and standing facing a wall, for good.
2. **A kept one can only leave by taking your place.** If it reaches you and touches you, you swap. You face the wall,
   and it walks out as you and "finishes your shift". That's the Part 2 STAY IN ending.
3. **It only moves when nobody is watching it,** meaning in the dark or while you look away. Light is safety. Your phone
   (2 % in Part 1) and later your flashlight are the light, so they're the season's resource.
4. **The closer it gets, the more it looks like you.** It learns you from reflections (the elevator's steel, the water,
   dark screens), so its copy of you is **mirrored**. This is the finale's fair-play clue.
5. **The one in the elevator was last night's worker,** a tall man in a grey suit (D. HALE, 20 Nov). He followed you
   through the office in the dark, which is why the lights went out behind you one row at a time in Part 1. Then he
   stood in the elevator corner ("someone you never saw get in"). He faces the wall so he can watch you in the steel.
6. **The building prints the rules; the kept ones scratch them out.** The printed text is always true.
   - Rule 2 in full: "IF SOMEONE IS FACING THE WALL, NEVER LOOK AWAY FROM THEM."
   - Rule 3: "THE NIGHT SHIFT ALWAYS KEEPS ONE."

## 3. The four engines (why each episode stays 50/50 and gets argued about)
| Engine | What viewers learn to track | What it gives the comments |
|---|---|---|
| Exact wording | Each rule's precise words | Loophole arguments ("zero WAS on the panel", "reaching in isn't stepping in") |
| Unseen movement | Dark, flicker or looking away means it moves | "It moves when the lights flicker!" theories, rewatching flickers |
| Becoming you | Height, lanyard, sweater, hair, face | "Did anyone notice it's shorter now?" spot-the-change |
| Clock + light | HUD countdown to 06:00, phone %, then the flashlight | Urgency; a real ticking clock in every episode |

Surface outcomes are balanced. Across the season the obey option wins 3 times and the break option wins 2, plus 2
puzzle choices. The real rule is always "read the words".

## 7. Episodes
Each sequel follows the sequel beat sheet: cold open (1 s), recap, losing branch, "Let's rewind.", winning branch, new
floor, new choice, question. Target 90-100 s (~250-280 words). The cold-open text is on screen from frame 1. The first
spoken line starts by 1.2 s and is ≤ 10 words.

### Part 2: "The Night Shift" (floor 0). Re-cut of the rendered episode; edits in §8.
- **Cold open:** the doors open and it's one step closer. Text: "IT MOVED. NOBODY SAW IT."
- **Recap:** "So, did you step out, or did you stay in?"
- **② STAY IN (loses):**
  1. The doors shut on the two of you, and the panel dies.
  2. It speaks without turning: "Don't worry. I'll finish your shift."
  3. "The lights go out. And in the dark, you hear it move."
  4. Lights on: it's gone, and you're the one facing the wall. "This is the end."
- **"Let's rewind."**
- **① STEP OUT (continues):**
  1. A 1 s insert as you step out: the panel, with "0" lit. This is the silent clue that zero was on the panel.
  2. The doors close and the car falls to a crash far below.
  3. Relief, then every monitor wakes: "WELCOME TO THE NIGHT SHIFT / 7 FLOORS · 1 RULE EACH / THE ONLY WAY OUT IS DOWN
     / CLOCK OUT BEFORE 06:00" (HUD starts).
  4. A ding: the car is back, with it inside, one step closer. "And nobody saw it move."
  5. The stairwell door creaks open, with footsteps coming up.
- **Choice:**
  - ① STAIRS (into the dark, toward the footsteps);
  - ② ELEVATOR (ride all the way down with it).
- **Loophole for the comments:** the half-legible Rule 2, "NEVER LO…". Look AT it, or look AWAY?
- **Last line:** "So, which way down do you go?"

### Part 3: "Don't Step in the Water" (0 → -1)
- **Cold open:** -1, your office flooded ankle-deep with black, still water. Your reflection in it is the back of your
  head. Text: "YOUR REFLECTION ISN'T FACING YOU."
- **Recap:** "So, did you take the stairs, or the elevator?" If we have real numbers, show the split.
- **Community callback** (only with a real comment): "A lot of you noticed: zero was on the panel."
- **① STAIRS (loses):**
  1. Red dark. The footsteps stop on the landing just below.
  2. Your phone light, 1 %, finds someone facing the wall: another one. "There's more than one."
  3. You squeeze past it, and for one second you look away.
  4. The red lamp dies. "This is the end."
- **"Let's rewind."**
- **② ELEVATOR (continues):**
  1. You step in and keep your eyes on its back. "Whatever that rule said, you're not looking away."
  2. The light flickers. When it comes back, it's a step closer. Another flicker, another step.
  3. In the brushed steel, its reflection is turned toward you, two pale eyes. The face stays unreadable.
  4. By -1 it's right in front of you. "It's your height now. And it's wearing a red lanyard. Like yours."
  5. Doors open. You back out without blinking, and the doors close on it.
- **Floor -1:**
  - Your office, flooded. The sign on the doors: "-1. DON'T STEP IN THE WATER."
  - The stairwell is on the far side, and the elevator panel is dead.
  - At the far end, the lights start switching off, one row at a time, coming toward you. This is the Part 1 callback:
    darkness is how it moves.
- **Choice:**
  - ① WADE (fast; it breaks the rule);
  - ② CLIMB THE DESKS (slow; the dark is coming).
- **Loophole:** "It only says STEP in the water."
- **Easter egg:** the reflection world has one extra person standing between the desks.
- **Last line:** "So, do you wade through the water, or climb across the desks?"

### Part 4: "Never Turn Around" (-1 → -3)
- **Cold open:** a wall of monitors, every one showing you from behind, and it standing right behind you. Text: "IT'S
  RIGHT BEHIND YOU. DON'T TURN."
- **Recap:** "So, did you wade, or did you climb?"
- **① WADE (loses):** The water is warm. Your reflection doesn't copy your step. It looks up at you, and pulls. "This is
  the end."
- **"Let's rewind."**
- **② CLIMB (continues):**
  1. Desk to desk. A chair rolls away, and the next desk tips.
  2. Your badge slips into the water, and you need it to clock out.
  3. "The rule says step. It doesn't say reach." You reach in.
  4. Under the surface, a hand closes around your right wrist. You tear free with the badge.
  5. Insert: a grey handprint on your RIGHT wrist. This is the finale clue, planted.
  6. The last row of lights dies just as you reach the stairwell.
- **Stairs:** a 1 s landing sign: "-2. IF YOU HEAR TYPING, DON'T STOP WALKING." The door behind it is bricked up.
- **Floor -3:**
  - Your office again, every monitor on, all showing the same live CCTV: you, from behind, in this room, right now.
  - The rule: "-3. NEVER TURN AROUND."
  - On the screens, it stands one step behind you. It isn't facing the wall any more. It's facing you, and it's
    wearing a blue sweater.
- **Choice:**
  - ① TURN AROUND;
  - ② KEEP WALKING (with it at your back).
- **Loophole:** "Watch it on the screens!" Does a camera count as looking?
- **Last line:** "So, do you turn around… or keep walking?"

### Part 5: "Every Night, One" (-3 → -5). The mythology episode.
- **Cold open:** a wall of framed photos of people from behind, each facing a wall, and one empty frame. Text: "EVERY
  NIGHT, IT KEEPS ONE."
- **Recap:** "So, did you turn around, or keep walking?"
- **① TURN AROUND (loses, short):**
  1. Nothing behind you, but on the screens it's still behind you.
  2. You turn again, and it's still behind you.
  3. The monitors die one by one. "This is the end."
- **"Let's rewind."**
- **② KEEP WALKING (continues):**
  1. You walk the rows with your eyes on the monitors. It stays exactly one step behind, and it never moves while you're
     watching.
  2. Callback slot: "Some of you said: watch the screens."
  3. The last screen goes to static, and you dive through the stairwell door.
- **Stairs:** a landing sign: "-4. COUNT THE CHAIRS. IF THERE IS ONE MORE, LEAVE."
- **Floor -5:**
  - Your office, but every wall is covered in framed photos, like employee of the month.
  - Every photo is someone from behind, facing a wall, with a name and a date, one for every night back to 1979.
  - Yesterday's photo: a tall man in a grey suit, "D. HALE · 20 NOV". "He was last night's last one leaving."
  - The newest frame is empty. Its plate says "YOU · 21 NOV".
  - On your desk: the original rules card, unscratched. "If someone is facing the wall, never look away from them."
    Rule 3 has been cut out.
  - The door rule: "-5. TAKE NOTHING THAT ISN'T YOURS."
  - In your drawer: the flashlight you always keep there. Your phone: 1 %.
- **Choice:**
  - ① TAKE THE FLASHLIGHT;
  - ② LEAVE IT.
- **Loophole:** is a copy of your desk yours?
- **Last line:** "So, do you take the flashlight, or leave it?"

### Part 6: "Two of You" (-5 → -7)
- **Cold open:** the punch clock at 05:57, and two of you, side by side. Text: "05:57. THERE ARE TWO OF YOU."
- **Recap:** "So, did you take it, or leave it?"
- **② LEAVE IT (loses):**
  1. The phone light carries you down to -6, then dies.
  2. In the dark, footsteps. Yours, except you've stopped walking. "This is the end."
- **"Let's rewind."**
- **① TAKE IT (continues):**
  1. "It's your desk. So it's yours." The beam pins it in place, and whenever the beam leaves it, it moves.
  2. Landing sign: "-6. IF IT CALLS YOUR NAME, DON'T ANSWER."
- **Floor -7:**
  - Your office, emptied. Along every wall, all of them, dozens, facing the wall: every night since 1979.
  - In the middle, a punch clock and a card with your name. Rule: "-7. ONLY ONE OF YOU CAN CLOCK OUT."
  - Your phone lights up one last time. Home: "OK. See you soon ❤". It's a reply to a message from your number,
    "Leaving now", that you never sent. Then the phone dies.
  - The flashlight flickers and goes out. Darkness, then footsteps right beside you.
  - The emergency light stutters on: two of you at the clock. Same sweater, same lanyard, same face. The face reveal
    promised in Part 1's notes: its face is yours.
  - Shell game: each flicker, both of you have moved.
- **Choice:**
  - ① LEFT;
  - ② RIGHT.
  - "Look closely before you answer."
- **Fair-play clue:** the handprint is on the right wrist on ①, and on the left wrist on ② (the mirrored copy).
- **Last line:** "So… which one is you?"

### Part 7: "Clock Out" (-7 → G, finale)
- **Cold open:** morning light through the lobby doors, and a grey sweater in the elevator corner. Text: "THE BUILDING
  ALWAYS KEEPS ONE."
- **Recap:** "So, which one did you pick?" Show the real split.
- **② RIGHT (loses):**
  1. It punches the card: 05:59. The doors open on G, and it walks out into the morning.
  2. Your phone, its phone, buzzes: "See you soon ❤".
  3. At -7, the real you turns to the wall, and the blue drains out of your sweater. "This is the end."
- **"Let's rewind."**
- **① LEFT (wins):**
  1. "Look at its wrist." Close-up: the handprint is on the left. Yours is on the right.
  2. "It learned you from reflections. The steel, the water, the dark screens. It got everything right. Except it
     learned you backwards."
  3. You punch your card: 05:59:59. Behind you, at 06:00, it turns to the wall and goes grey.
  4. G. Morning. One bar of signal. Home: "Where are you??" You type: "On my way."
- **Final turn:** above the punch clock, the full card. "3. THE NIGHT SHIFT ALWAYS KEEPS ONE."
- **Epilogue (loop):**
  1. The next night, 23:59. Someone else is the last one leaving the office.
  2. Behind them, the lights switch off, one row at a time.
  3. In the elevator corner, facing the wall: someone in a grey sweater with a red lanyard, and a handprint on the
     left wrist.
- **Last line**, word for word the first line of Part 1: "Imagine you're the last one leaving the office."
- **Pinned comment:** "Watch Part 1 again. Now you know who turned off the lights."

## 9. Viral rules applied (sources)
- **Vyral `viral-youtube-shorts`:**
  - The first 3 s decide the swipe-away rate.
  - No logo or intro.
  - A turn or stakes raise at 5-8 s: our losing branch turns by ~6 s.
  - Loop design: Part 7 ends on Part 1's first line, and every last frame is the next part's recap frame.
  - Captions in the middle safe zone.
  - No "subscribe" CTA.
- **Vyral `viral-hooks` (three-layer hook):**
  - Frame 1 shows the conflict, not a waiting face.
  - The spoken line is 5-10 words and lands before 2 s.
  - On-screen text is 3-7 words, sharpens rather than repeats, and works muted.
  - Match the promise to the payoff: every cold-open image is delivered in that episode.
- **rediumvex hooks:**
  - "POV / what would you do if" (068/057).
  - Specific-time hooks (082): "05:57. THERE ARE TWO OF YOU."
- **novincode story-craft / psychology:**
  - Every beat turns ("but/therefore", never "and then").
  - At least one loop open at every moment; close small loops often and the big loop last.
  - Every twist violates a prediction and is planted first. The mirror clue is planted in Parts 3, 4 and 6 before
    Part 7.
  - Specific, personal stakes: the "See you soon ❤" text.
  - The ending is surprising and inevitable.
- **artemnovitckii storytelling / hooks:**
  - Write the last line first. The "last dab" loops into the first.
  - CTAs close, never open: the COMMENT pill appears only on the final question.
- **create-viral-content:**
  - Episode titles must score ≥ 7/9 on curiosity, specificity and emotion.
  - High-arousal fear drives sharing.
  - Keep the series clearly fiction.
