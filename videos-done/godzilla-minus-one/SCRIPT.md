## Facts (checked 2026-11-19)
- **Castle Bravo / Lucky Dragon No. 5:** the U.S. Castle Bravo H-bomb test at Bikini Atoll on March 1, 1954 dropped
  fallout on the Japanese tuna boat Daigo Fukuryū Maru (Lucky Dragon No. 5), 23 crew. They saw the flash in the west
  before dawn, white coral ash fell on the deck for hours, and they called it *shi no hai*, "death ash". Radio
  operator Aikichi Kuboyama died on September 23, 1954. (Wikipedia, Daigo Fukuryū Maru; Civil Beat, "Bikini Atoll:
  the day the sun rose in the west", 2015; history.com, Godzilla 1954 inspiration)
- **Godzilla (1954):** directed by Ishirō Honda, special effects by Eiji Tsuburaya; producer Tomoyuki Tanaka took the
  idea from the Lucky Dragon news. Opened in Nagoya on October 27 and nationwide on November 3, 1954, eight months
  after the test. "Gojira" blends *gorira* (gorilla) and *kujira* (whale). Godzilla was "suitmation": stuntman
  Haruo Nakajima in a rubber suit of roughly 100 kg (about 220 lb) on miniature Tokyo sets. (Wikipedia, Godzilla
  franchise / Godzilla (1954 film); motionpictures.org; neatorama "How to Make a Monster")
- **Franchise:** Shōwa (1954–1975), Heisei (1984–1995), Millennium (1999–2004), Reiwa (2016–). From *Ghidorah, the
  Three-Headed Monster* (1964) he turned into a friendlier antihero who defends Earth from other monsters (complete by
  *Son of Godzilla*, 1967). Guinness: "longest continuously running film series". 38 films before Minus Zero (it is
  the 39th: 34 Japanese + 5 American). (Wikipedia, Godzilla (franchise); Godzilla Minus Zero)
- **Godzilla Minus One (2023):** written, directed and VFX-supervised by Takashi Yamazaki; released November 3, 2023
  (70th anniversary). Title: postwar Japan was at zero and Godzilla pushes it to minus one (Yamazaki). Kōichi
  Shikishima, a kamikaze pilot, lands on Odo Island claiming engine trouble (he faked it); Godzilla attacks, he
  freezes and fails to fire, and only he and mechanic Tachibana survive. In burned-out Tokyo he takes in Noriko and
  the orphan baby Akiko and works on a minesweeper. Godzilla, mutated by U.S. nuclear tests at Bikini Atoll, levels
  Ginza; Noriko is caught in the blast. Ex-navy engineer Kenji Noda's civilian plan (no government help): sink
  Godzilla fast in a ring of Freon gas tanks, then haul him up fast with balloons so the pressure change kills him.
  Shikishima flies a Shinden fighter packed with explosives into Godzilla's mouth, but Tachibana fitted an ejection
  seat and he ejects. Noriko survived, with a black mark spreading on her neck; Godzilla's flesh regenerates as it
  sinks. (Wikipedia, Godzilla Minus One)
- **Budget and Oscar:** budget reported around $10–15 million (Yamazaki: within $10–15 million), VFX team of about
  35. Won Best Visual Effects at the 96th Academy Awards (March 10, 2024), the franchise's first Oscar, beating
  Hollywood tentpoles; the team brought small Godzilla figures on stage. (Wikipedia; Slashfilm; The Hollywood
  Reporter; Popverse "Godzilla Minus One Oscars win VFX team toys")
- **Godzilla Minus Zero (2026):** 39th Godzilla film, sequel by Yamazaki; premiered at the New York Film Festival on
  September 26, 2026; released in Japan November 3, 2026, US/Canada November 6, 2026 (GKIDS). Set in 1949, two years
  after Minus One; the Shikishima family returns. Trailers tease golden lightning and a three-headed rival (King
  Ghidorah). First Japanese production filmed for IMAX. (Wikipedia, Godzilla Minus Zero; Collider; Den of Geek;
  NYFF)

No Minus Zero plot spoilers are used: only its setting and what the trailers show. Minus One is recapped in full,
because the video is the "watch this first" catch-up.
