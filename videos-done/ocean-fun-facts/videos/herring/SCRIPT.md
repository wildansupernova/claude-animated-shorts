# "The Navy That Hunted Fish Farts": script v1 (2026-11-15)
Format: ~65 s vertical short, viral formula: paradox hook → setting → escalation → "Wait...?" mystery → "You see," mechanism + everyday analogy → ironic callback payoff.
Voice: Kokoro `am_puck`, per-line speed changes for expressive delivery. Captions: 1-3 words, active word highlighted, emphasis words coloured.

## Facts (sources)
- Oct 1981: Soviet Whiskey-class submarine S-363 ran aground just outside a big Swedish naval base (Karlskrona), the "Whiskey on the Rocks" affair. (Irish Times 2026-05-14; IFLScience)
- Sweden became convinced Soviet subs were operating in its archipelagos, invested heavily in sonar; sonar picked up unidentified bubbling/ticking noises; for over a decade Sweden tried and failed to identify the submarines. (Irish Times)
- 1996: puzzled the "submarines" were still active after the Soviet Union collapsed (1991), Sweden invited two biologists to listen to the recordings (Magnus Wahlberg, Håkan Westerberg). (Irish Times; Improbable Research)
- Herring have swim bladders connected to the gut (they gulp air at the surface); they release gas from the rear in rapid bursts of bubbles; on a hydrophone it is a high-frequency buzzing that sounds like a small submersible's propulsion. Sounds noted at night. (Irish Times; Wilson, Batty & Dill 2004, Biology Letters; SAMS)
- A British/Canadian team (Wilson, Batty, Dill) independently named them "Fast Repetitive Ticks" = FRTs. (SAMS; Nature news 2004)
- Both teams shared the 2004 Ig Nobel Prize in Biology. (Nature news, 1 Oct 2004; Improbable Research)

## Script (≈220 words)
1. For over ten years, Sweden's navy hunted Soviet submarines... that turned out to be fish farts.
2. It's 1981. A real Soviet submarine has just run aground, right outside a Swedish naval base.
3. Sweden is furious, so the navy fills the sea with microphones, and listens day and night.
4. Then they hear it. A strange, crackling hiss, like bacon frying under the water.
5. Submarine! Warships race out to hunt the intruder down... and find absolutely nothing.
6. But the hissing keeps coming back. Night after night. Year after year.
7. The Cold War ends. The Soviet Union collapses. But the submarines are still hissing.
8. Wait. Who's still driving Soviet submarines, when there's no Soviet Union?
9. So in 1996, the navy finally plays the tapes to two biologists.
10. You see, it's not a submarine. It's herring.
11. Herring gulp air at the surface and keep it in a swim bladder, a little balloon connected to their gut.
12. So when they let the air out, it comes out the back, in tiny, rapid bubbles.
13. Ever let the air squeak out of a balloon? Now picture thousands of fish doing it at once.
14. On sonar, that sounds a lot like a tiny submarine engine.
15. Scientists even gave it an official name: Fast Repetitive Tick. F. R. T.
16. The discovery won an Ig Nobel Prize.
17. So for years, an entire navy was at war... with fish farts.
