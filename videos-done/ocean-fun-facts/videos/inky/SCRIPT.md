# "Inky's Great Escape": script v1 (2026-11-15)
Format: ~60 s vertical short. Hook (prison break, nobody saw a thing) → setting (Inky, Napier aquarium, scarred rescue) → escalation (lid left ajar, squeeze, cross the floor, find the drain, pour in, 50 m to the sea) → discovery (empty tank, wet trail) → "Wait...?" (rugby ball vs 15 cm pipe) → "You see," (no bones, only the beak is hard) + "bag of jelly with a brain" → smarts → payoff (never seen again; tank mate Blotchy stayed behind).

## Facts (sources)
- Inky, a male common New Zealand octopus at the National Aquarium of New Zealand in Napier; caught by fishermen on Pania Reef and donated in 2014, "battle-worn and scarred". Staff: "very inquisitive and liked to push boundaries". (National Geographic 2016; Boston Globe)
- During routine cleaning the lid of his tank was left slightly ajar; at night he got out, crossed about 2.5 m of floor (wet tracks), and went down a ~15 cm (6 in) wide drain pipe about 50 m long that empties into Hawke's Bay. Found out next morning; made public in April 2016 about three months later. (NPR; Time; Live Science; Irish Times; Boston Globe)
- His tank mate, Blotchy, stayed behind. (Irish Times / Shortlist)
- Octopuses have soft, boneless bodies and can squeeze through tiny openings (the beak is the only hard part); they are intelligent and can recognise individual faces. (National Geographic; Live Science)
