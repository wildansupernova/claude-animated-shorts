# The Forest Snitch — script (our own story)

## Facts (sources)
- Decomposing remains release nitrogen (and other nutrients) into the soil; plants above can respond (higher δ15N, greener / denser leaves).
  Trends in Plant Science 2020 (Ceballos, DeBruyn, Stewart et al., Univ. of Tennessee) proposed plant "sentinels" + drones that read leaf reflectance:
  https://www.axios.com/2020/09/05/plants-people-forensic-botanists , https://modernfarmer.com/2020/09/plants-could-help-find-dead-bodies/
- Grave-indicator vegetation: soil loosened and aerated by digging grows plants more vigorously; disturbance alone changes the plant cover;
  "abnormally strong growth 10 months after" (Watson et al., J Forensic Sci 2020): https://zenodo.org/record/4297169/files/WatsonEtAl_GraveDetectionBotanyAFTER_JFS%202020.pdf
- Depressions: "some depression may occur as a result of body decomposition" (slow): https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Hartnell_College/The_Introduction_to_the_Forensic_Sciences/09%3A_Forensic_Entomology/9.04%3A_Forensic_Taphonomy_and_Clandestine_Grave_Detection
- Ammonia fungi fruit after nitrogen (urea / ammonia) reaches the soil, incl. from decomposing remains; Hebeloma syrjense is
  "often referred to as the corpse finder": https://dannyhaelewaters.com/forensic-mycology-taking-hebeloma-to-court ,
  https://davidmoore.org.uk/assets/fungi4schools/Reprints/Mycologist_articles/Post-16/Environment/V17pp020-024forensic_fungal_ecology.pdf
- Dog-urine lawn spots: concentrated nitrogen (urea) burns the grass brown, the diluted edge grows a darker green ring (classic lawn-care fact).
- Search teams look for vegetation / soil anomalies; satellite vegetation-stress detection reached ~9 y 11 m after burial (Bournemouth PhD 2019).

## Beats
Hook -> setting (Gary) -> action x4 (brown, greener, mushrooms, sinking) -> Luckily / Unluckily -> why (nitrogen, dog-pee analogy, corpse finder)
-> consequence (search teams, drones) -> callback twist (picnic).

## Voice (one steady tone)
1. If you bury a body in the woods, the forest will snitch on you.
2. Meet Gary. Gary has a shovel, a very heavy rug, and no plan B.
3. He finds a quiet clearing, digs a hole, rolls the rug in, and puts the grass back on top. Perfect.
4. A few weeks later, the grass on top turns brown. In a rectangle.
5. Then it grows back. Greener. Way greener. The greenest rectangle in the whole forest.
6. Then mushrooms start popping up. Only there.
7. And then the ground slowly sinks, into the shape of a grave.
8. Luckily for Gary, nobody saw him dig. Unluckily, the forest is now pointing right at it.
9. Here's why. Digging loosens the soil, and a body is full of nitrogen. Basically, fertilizer.
10. It's like a dog peeing on your lawn. Too much nitrogen burns the grass brown, then it grows back greener than everything around it.
11. Some mushrooms only show up where rotting things leak nitrogen. One is literally nicknamed the corpse finder.
12. That's why search teams hunt for plants that don't match. And scientists want drones that spot leaves a little too green.
13. So if you ever find a perfectly green rectangle in the woods, maybe have your picnic somewhere else.
